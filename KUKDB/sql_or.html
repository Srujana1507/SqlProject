OR Operator

The OR operator in SQL is used to combine multiple conditions in a query.


It returns results where at least one of the conditions evaluates to TRUE.


Key Features of OR Operator

  • Combines Multiple Conditions:- Enables flexibility by allowing at least one condition to be satisfied.
  • Used in WHERE, ON, and HAVING Clauses:- Commonly used to filter data in these clauses.
  • Handles NULL Values:- If one of the conditions evaluates to NULL and another evaluates to TRUE, the result is TRUE.
  • Execution Order:- The OR operator is evaluated after AND unless parentheses are used to change the precedence.

Syntax

SELECT column1 , column2 , . . .
FROM table_name
WHERE condition1 OR condition2 ;

Examples

Scenario 1:- Retrieve employees who work in department 10 or department 20.

SELECT employee_id , department_id , first_name
FROM employees
WHERE department_id = 10 OR department_id = 20 ;

Scenario 2:- Retrieve customers who are located in either 'Hyderabad' or 'Mumbai'.

SELECT customer_id , customer_name , City
FROM customers
WHERE city = 'Hyderabad' OR city = 'Mumbai' ;

Scenario 3:- Retrieve products in the "Electronics" or "Clothing" category.

SELECT ProductID , Name , Category
FROM Products
WHERE Category = 'Electronics' OR Category = 'Clothing' ;

Scenario 4:- Find orders placed in January 2024 or July 2024.

SELECT OrderID , OrderDate , Amount
FROM Orders
WHERE OrderDate BETWEEN '01-01-2024' AND '31-01-2024'
OR OrderDate BETWEEN '01-07-2024' AND '31-07-2024' ;

Scenario 5:- Get employees earning above $70,000 or working in the "Marketing" department.

SELECT EmployeeID , Name , department_name , Salary
FROM Employees
WHERE Salary > 70000 OR department_name ='Marketing' ;

Operator Precedence and Parentheses

The OR operator has lower precedence than the AND operator. Without parentheses, AND conditions will execute before OR.


Example Without Parentheses:

SELECT *
FROM Employees
WHERE department_id = 10 OR department_id = 20 AND salary > 50000 ; - - Execution Order:
  • department_id = 20 AND salary > 50000 is evaluated first.
  • Then, combined with department_id = 10 using OR.

Correcting with Parentheses:

SELECT *
FROM Employees
WHERE ( department_id = 10 OR department_id = 20 )AND salary > 50000 ; - - Execution Order:
  • (department_id = 10 OR department_id = 20) is evaluated first.
  • Then, combined with salary > 50000 using AND.

Handling NULL with OR

Scenario :-Retrieve products with a price greater than 100 or those without a price specified (NULL).

SELECT product_id , product_name , price
FROM products
WHERE price > 100 OR price IS NULL ;

Advanced Scenarios

1. Using OR with AND

Scenario 1:- Retrieve employees who are either in department 30 or earn more than 70,000 in salary but are not managers.

SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary , job_title
FROM employees
WHERE ( department_id = 30 OR salary > 70000 ) AND department_name != 'Manager' ;

2. Using OR in JOIN Conditions

Scenario :- Retrieve orders where the customer is located in 'New York' or the shipping address is in 'California'.

SELECT o.order_id , o.customer_id , c.city AS customer_city , s.state AS shipping_state
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
JOIN shipping s ON o.shipping_id = s.shipping_id
WHERE c.city = 'New York' OR s.state = 'California' ;

3. Using OR with EXISTS

Scenario :- Find employees who work in the 'Sales' department or have placed at least one order.

SELECT employee_id , department_id
FROM employees
WHERE department_id IN (
    SELECT department_id FROM departments WHERE department_name = 'Sales'
    )
OR EXISTS ( SELECT 1 FROM orders WHERE orders.employee_id = employees.employee_id ) ;

4. Combining Multiple OR Conditions

Scenario :- Retrieve orders with statuses "Pending," "Processing," or "Delayed."

SELECT OrderID , Status , OrderDate
FROM Orders
WHERE Status = 'Pending' OR Status = 'Processing' OR Status = 'Delayed' ;

5. Nested Conditions

Scenario :- Retrieve products priced below $50 or products in the "Electronics" category with a stock count of 0.

SELECT ProductID , Name , Category , Price , Stock
FROM Products
WHERE Price < 50
    OR ( Category = 'Electronics' AND Stock = 0 ) ;

Common Errors with OR

1. Unintended Results Due to Operator Precedence

  • Without parentheses, AND has higher precedence than OR.
WHERE A OR B AND C ; - - Evaluates as A OR ( B AND C ).

FIX :

WHERE ( A OR B ) AND C ;

2. Redundant Conditions

  • Adding conditions that overlap unnecessarily.
WHERE Category = 'Electronics' OR Category = 'Electronics' ;

3. NULL Values

  • OR evaluates NULL conditions carefully.
WHERE Column1 = 'Value' OR Column2 IS NULL ; - - Works correctly.

Best Practices for Using OR

    1. Use Parentheses:- Always group conditions explicitly to avoid ambiguity.

    2.Optimize Conditions:- Minimize the number of OR clauses by using sets like IN for better performance.
    - - Better approach:
    WHERE Status IN ( 'Pending' , 'Processing' , 'Delayed' ) ;

    3. Combine with Indexing:- Ensure columns involved in OR conditions are indexed for faster query execution.

    4. Avoid Redundancy:- Do not repeat conditions or include overlapping logic.

    5. Consider Query Optimization:- For large datasets, rewrite OR conditions as UNION queries for better performance in some databases:
    SELECT * FROM Table WHERE Condition1
    UNION
    SELECT * FROM Table WHERE Condition2 ;

Common Use Cases

    1. Filter Data Across Multiple Categories:- Example: Find orders placed by VIP customers or during promotional events.

    2. Query Data from Related Columns:- Example: Search for employees with certain skill sets or certifications.

    3. Simplify User Input Filters:- Example: Allow users to search for products by multiple attributes, such as category or brand.
