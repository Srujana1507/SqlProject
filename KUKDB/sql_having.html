HAVING


  • The HAVING clause is used to filter the results of grouped data after applying aggregate functions.
  • While the WHERE clause filters rows before grouping, the HAVING clause filters groups created by the GROUP BY clause.
  • It is typically used in combination with the GROUP BY clause.
  • The HAVING clause applies conditions to aggregated data.

Syntax:

SELECT column1 ,aggregate_function(column2)
FROM table_name
WHERE condition
GROUP BY column1
HAVING condition ;

Key Points:

  • Purpose :- HAVING filters the results of a GROUP BY query.
  • Scope :- It works with aggregate functions such as SUM, AVG, COUNT, MIN, MAX.
  • Difference Between WHERE and HAVING
    • WHERE filters individual rows before grouping.
    • HAVING filters aggregated data after grouping.
  • Order of Execution :- FROM → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → ORDER BY

Scenarios

1. Basic Example

Retrieve departments with total sales greater than $10,000

SELECT department_name ,SUM(Sales) AS TotalSales
FROM Employees
GROUP BY department_name
HAVING SUM(Sales) > 10000 ;

- - Groups employees by department_name.
- - Filters out groups where the total salary exceeds 10000.

2. HAVING Without GROUP BY

Filter aggregated data when grouping is not required. Find the total sales where the amount exceeds 500,000

SELECT SUM(order_amount) AS total_sales
FROM orders
HAVING SUM(order_amount) > 500000 ;

- - Aggregates the entire table into one result (SUM(order_amount)).
- - Filters the result using HAVING.

3. HAVING with Multiple Conditions

Filter aggregated data when grouping is not required. Find total sales where the amount exceeds 500,000

SELECT department_id , SUM(salary) AS total_salary,
COUNT( * ) AS employee_count
FROM employees
GROUP BY department_id
HAVING SUM(salary) > 100000 AND COUNT( * ) > 10 ;

- Groups employees by department_id.
- - Filters out groups that do not meet both conditions.

4. Combining WHERE and HAVING

Retrieve departments with employees earning more than $5000 and total department sales greater than $15,000.

SELECT department_id , SUM(Sales) AS TotalSales
FROM employees
WHERE Salary > 5000
GROUP BY department_id
HAVING SUM( Sales ) > 15000 ;

- - WHERE filters individual rows before grouping.
- - HAVING filters the grouped results.

5. Using Aliases in HAVING

Find customers whose total order amount exceeds 1,000.

SELECT customer_id , SUM(order_amount) AS total_spent
FROM orders
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING SUM(order_amount) > 1000 ;

- - Uses the alias total_spent in the HAVING clause for clarity.

6. HAVING with NULLs

Find departments where the total salary exceeds 100,000, including departments with NULL salaries.

SELECT department_id , SUM(salary) AS total_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department_id
HAVING SUM(salary) > 100000 OR SUM(salary) IS NULL ;

- - Includes groups where the sum of salaries is NULL.

Advanced Scenarios

1. Using Subqueries in HAVING

Retrieve departments with total sales greater than the average department sales.

SELECT department_name , SUM(Sales) AS TotalSales
FROM Employees
GROUP BY department_name
HAVING SUM(Sales) >
(
    SELECT AVG(dept_total)
    FROM ( SELECT SUM(sales) AS dept_total
    FROM Employees
    GROUP BY department_name
) AS dept_avgs) ;

- - The subquery calculates the average department sales.
- - The HAVING clause filters groups based on this calculated value.

2. Filtering with Aggregate Functions on Multiple Columns

Retrieve products where the total sales quantity is greater than twice the number of distinct customers.

SELECT ProductID , SUM( Quantity ) AS TotalQuantity ,
COUNT( DISTINCT CustomerID) AS UniqueCustomers
FROM Sales
GROUP BY ProductID
HAVING SUM(Quantity) > 2 * COUNT( DISTINCT CustomerID) ;

3. Using Window Functions with HAVING

Retrieve departments where the total sales are in the top 50% of all departments.

WITH DepartmentSales AS
(
    SELECT department_name , SUM( Quantity ) AS TotalSales ,
    PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY SUM(Sales) DESC) AS Rank
    FROM Employees
    GROUP BY department_name
)
SELECT department_name , TotalSales
FROM DepartmentSales
WHERE Rank <= 0.5 ;

- - Window functions like PERCENT_RANK() can be used to calculate rankings.
- - Filtering is done in the main query after grouping.

4. Combining HAVING with Complex Expressions

Retrieve departments where the average salary is greater than the overall company average salary.

SELECT department_name , AVG( Salary ) AS AvgSalary
FROM Employee
GROUP BY Department
HAVING AVG( Salary ) > ( SELECT AVG(Salary) FROM Employee ) ;

Complex Scenarios

1. Sales Analytics: High-Performing Products

Scenario : Identify products sold in at least 3 different regions and whose total revenue exceeds the average revenue of all products.

SELECT ProductID, SUM(Revenue) AS TotalRevenue ,
COUNT ( DISTINCT Region ) AS RegionCount
FROM Sales
GROUP BY ProductID
HAVING
COUNT( DISTINCT Region) >= 3 AND SUM(Revenue) > ( SELECT AVG(Revenue)
FROM Sales );

- - COUNT(DISTINCT Region) >= 3 ensures products were sold in at least three regions.
- - The subquery calculates the average revenue across all products, and the HAVING clause compares each product’s revenue against this value.

2. HR Analytics: Identifying Top-Performing Departments

Scenario : List departments where the average employee salary is above $70,000, and the number of employees earning less than $50,000 is fewer than 5.

SELECT department_name, AVG( Salary ) AS AvgSalary, COUNT(* ) AS TotalEmployees
FROM Employees
GROUP BY department_name
HAVING AVG(Salary) > 70000 AND SUM( CASE WHEN Salary < 50000 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END ) < 5 ;

- - AVG(Salary) > 70000 filters departments with high average salaries.
- - The SUM(CASE WHEN ...) counts employees earning less than $50,000, ensuring the count is under 5.

3. Customer Insights: Loyal Customers

Scenario : Find customers who have made purchases totaling more than $5,000 and placed orders in at least 5 different months.

SELECT CustomerID , SUM (OrderAmount) AS TotalSpent , COUNT(DISTINCT MONTH(OrderDate)) AS ActiveMonths
FROM Orders
GROUP BY CustomerID
HAVING SUM(OrderAmount) > 5000
AND COUNT( DISTINCT MONTH (OrderDate) ) >= 5 ;

- - SUM(OrderAmount) > 5000 ensures high spenders are identified.
- - COUNT(DISTINCT MONTH(OrderDate)) >= 5 ensures the customer was active in at least 5 months.

4. Finance: Risky Branch Analysis

Scenario : Identify bank branches where the total loan amount exceeds $1 million, but at least 20% of loans are overdue.

SELECT BranchID , SUM( LoanAmount ) AS TotalLoans ,
    SUM( CASE WHEN Status = 'Overdue' THEN LoanAmount ELSE 0 END ) AS OverdueLoans ,
    (SUM( CASE WHEN Status = 'Overdue' THEN LoanAmount ELSE 0 END ) * 100.0 / SUM(LoanAmount)) AS OverduePercentage
FROM Loans
GROUP BY BranchID
HAVING SUM( LoanAmount ) > 1000000 AND
(SUM( CASE WHEN Status = 'Overdue' THEN LoanAmount ELSE 0 END ) * 100.0 / SUM(LoanAmount))> 20 ;

- - Calculates the total overdue loans and their percentage.
- - Filters branches based on the total loan amount and overdue percentage.

5. Inventory Management: Overstocked Items

Scenario : Find products with inventory levels exceeding 150% of their average monthly sales in the past year.

SELECT ProductID ,
SUM(Inventory) AS TotalInventory ,
AVG( SalesPerMonth ) AS AvgMonthlySales
FROM
(
    SELECT ProductID , Inventory , SUM(Sales) OVER (PARTITION BY ProductID) / 12 AS SalesPerMonth
    FROM InventoryData
) AS MonthlyData
GROUP BY ProductID
HAVING SUM( Inventory ) > 1.5 * AVG( SalesPerMonth ) ;


- - AVG(SalesPerMonth) calculates the average monthly sales for each product.
- - SUM(Inventory) > 1.5 * AVG(SalesPerMonth) ensures only overstocked products are included.

6. Event Management: Popular Event Locations

Scenario : Find venues that hosted more than 3 events in the last year and had an average attendance above 500.

SELECT VenueID , COUNT(*) AS EventCount ,
AVG(Attendance) AS AvgAttendance
FROM Events WHERE EventDate >= DATEADD( YEAR , -1 , GETDATE())
GROUP BY VenueID
HAVING COUNT(*) > 3 AND AVG(Attendance) > 500 ;

- - COUNT(*) > 3 ensures only venues hosting more than 3 events are included.
- - AVG(Attendance) > 500 filters for high-attendance venues.

7. Logistics: Delayed Shipments Analysis

Scenario : Find venues that hosted more than 3 events in the last year and had an average attendance above 500.

SELECT RouteID , COUNT(*) AS ShipmentCount ,
AVG(DATEDIFF( DAY , ExpectedDeliveryDate , ActualDeliveryDate)) AS AvgDelay
FROM Shipments
WHERE DATEDIFF( DAY , ExpectedDeliveryDate , ActualDeliveryDate) > 0
GROUP BY RouteID
HAVING COUNT(*) > 20 AND AVG(DATEDIFF( DAY , ExpectedDeliveryDate , ActualDeliveryDate)) >0 ;

- - Filters routes with more than 20 shipments.
- - Ensures that the average delay for these routes exceeds 5 days.

8. Healthcare: Frequent Patients

Scenario : Identify patients who visited more than 10 times in the past year and whose average bill exceeded $1,000.

SELECT PatientID , COUNT(*) AS VisitCount ,
AVG(BillAmount) AS AvgBill
FROM PatientVisits
WHERE VisitDate >= DATEADD( YEAR , -1 , GETDATE())
GROUP BY PatientID
HAVING COUNT(*) > 10 AND AVG(BillAmount) > 1000 ;

- - COUNT(*) > 10 filters patients with frequent visits.
- - AVG(BillAmount) > 1000 ensures the patient’s bills are significant.

Common Errors with HAVING

1. Error: "Invalid use of aggregate function in WHERE clause"

  • Cause: Using an aggregate function in the WHERE clause.
  • Fix: Move the aggregate function to the HAVING clause.
- - Incorrect SELECT department_name , SUM(Sales)
FROM Employees
WHERE SUM(Sales) > 10000 ;

- - Correct SELECT department_name , SUM(Sales)
FROM Employees
GROUP BY department_name
HAVING SUM(Sales) > 10000 ;

2. Error: "Column must appear in the GROUP BY clause or be used in an aggregate function"

  • Cause : A column in the SELECT clause is neither in GROUP BY nor in an aggregate function.
  • Fix : Add the column to GROUP BY or use an aggregate function.

Best Practices for HAVING

1. Optimize Filtering with WHERE and HAVING

  • Use WHERE to filter rows early and reduce the number of rows processed by the query.
  • Use HAVING only for conditions on aggregated results.

2. Simplify Complex Queries

  • Use subqueries, CTEs, or views to break down complex filtering.

3. Use Aliases and CTEs for Clarity

  • Break down complex queries using aliases and Common Table Expressions (CTEs) for better readability.

4. Combine Conditions Carefully

  • Ensure proper use of logical operators (AND, OR) with parentheses if needed.
HAVING SUM(salary) > 100000 AND (COUNT(*) > 10 OR MIN(hire_date) < '2020-01-01') ;

5. Understand NULL Behavior

  • Aggregate functions like SUM or COUNT behave differently with NULL values, so consider handling them explicitly.

6. Avoid Redundant Conditions

  • Avoid repeating the same condition in both WHERE and HAVING.
