AND

The AND operator returns TRUE only if all conditions are TRUE. If any condition is FALSE, the result is FALSE.


Syntax

SELECT column1 , column2 , . . .
FROM table_name
WHERE condition1 AND condition2 ;

Basic Scenarios

Scenario 1 - ( Filtering Employees by Department and Salary ):- Retrieve employees from department 10 with salaries greater than 50,000.

SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
FROM employees
WHERE department_id = 10 AND salary > 50000 ;

Scenario 2 - ( Finding Products with Specific Criteria ) :- Find products that are in the "Electronics" category and have a stock quantity greater than 100.

SELECT ProductID, Name, Category, Stock
FROM Products
WHERE Category = 'Electronics' AND Stock > 100 ;

Scenario 3 - ( Filtering Orders by Date and Status ) :- Retrieve all orders placed after January 1, 2023, that are still pending.

SELECT OrderID , CustomerID , OrderDate , Status
FROM Orders
WHERE OrderDate > '2023-01-01' AND Status = 'Pending' ;

Scenario 4 - ( Combining Numeric and String Conditions ):- Get all students enrolled in the "Mathematics" course with grades above 80.

SELECT StudentID , Name , Course , Grade
FROM Students
WHERE Course = 'Mathematics' AND Grade > 80 ;

Scenario 5 - ( Filtering Products by Multiple Numeric Ranges ):- Get all products priced between 50 and 500 with a discount greater than 10%.

SELECT ProductID , Name , Price , Discount
FROM Products
WHERE Price BETWEEN 50 AND 500 AND Discount > 10 ;

Scenario 6 - ( Combining Date and Textual Filters ):- Retrieve events that are scheduled after today and have the status "Confirmed."

SELECT EventID , Name , EventDate , Status
FROM Events
WHERE EventDate > CURRENT_DATE AND Status = 'Confirmed' ;

Operator Precedence

When multiple logical operators are used in a query, they are evaluated in the following order of precedence:NOT , AND , OR

Use parentheses to override the default precedence and clarify intent.


Example 1:

SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
FROM employees
WHERE department_id = 10 OR salary > 50000 AND NOT job_title = 'Manager' ; ---- add parenthesis for clarity.

To change precedence:

SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
FROM employees
WHERE ( department_id = 10 OR salary > 50000 ) AND NOT job_title = 'Manager' ; - - 1. Evaluate NOT job_title = 'Manager'.
- - 2. Evaluate salary > 50000 AND result_of_NOT.
- - 3. Evaluate department_id = 10 OR result_of_AND.

Example 2:

Retrieve employees from department 20 who are either earning more than 60,000 or have a job title other than 'Salesman'.

SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary , job_title
FROM employees
WHERE department_id = 20 AND ( salary > 60000 OR job_title != 'Salesman' ) ; - - 1. Evaluate salary > 60000 OR job_title != 'Salesman'
- - 2. Evaluate result of OR with department_id = 20 using AND

Advanced Scenarios

1. Combining AND with OR

Scenario :- Retrieve customers from "USA" or "Canada" who have spent more than $5,000.

SELECT CustomerID , Name , Country , TotalSpending
FROM Customers
WHERE ( Country = 'USA' OR Country = 'Canada' ) AND TotalSpending > 5000 ;

2. Joining Tables with AND in Conditions

Scenario :- Find employees who belong to "Sales" or "Marketing" departments and have completed at least two projects.

SELECT e.EmployeeID , e.Name , e.department_name , COUNT( p.ProjectID ) AS ProjectCount
FROM Employees e
JOIN Projects p ON e.EmployeeID = p.EmployeeID
WHERE (e.department_name = 'Sales' OR e.department_name = 'Marketing' )
AND p.Status = 'Completed'
GROUP BY e.EmployeeID , e.Name , e.department_name HAVING COUNT(p.ProjectID) >= 2 ;

3. Using Subqueries with AND

Scenario :- Retrieve orders placed by customers who live in "California" and have placed more than three orders.

SELECT OrderID , CustomerID , OrderDate , Amount
FROM Orders
WHERE CustomerID IN (
    SELECT CustomerID
    FROM Orders
    WHERE State = 'California'
    GROUP BY CustomerID
    HAVING COUNT(OrderID) > 3 );

Common Errors with AND

1. Misusing AND/OR

Using OR without parentheses can lead to unintended results due to operator precedence.

WHERE DepartmentID = 10 OR DepartmentID = 20 AND Salary > 60000 ; - - This will first evaluate DepartmentID = 20 AND Salary > 60000.

Fix

WHERE ( DepartmentID = 10 OR DepartmentID = 20 ) AND Salary > 60000 ;

2. Logical Conflicts

Conditions that contradict each other will return no rows.

WHERE Price < 50 AND Price > 100 ; - - Always false.

3. Null Handling in AND conditions

Conditions involving NULLs require special attention.

WHERE Category IS NOT NULL AND Stock > 0 ; - - Always false.
