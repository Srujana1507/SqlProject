    OR Operator in Oracle SQL

    The OR operator in Oracle SQL is a logical operator used to combine two or more conditions.


    • It returns TRUE if at least one of the conditions evaluates to TRUE.
    • The OR operator is typically used in the WHERE clause to broaden query results by specifying multiple criteria.

    Key Features of OR Operator

    Feature Description
    Combines Conditions Combines two or more conditions, any one of which must be TRUE for a row to be included.
    Evaluates Any Condition If one condition evaluates to TRUE, subsequent conditions are not evaluated (short-circuiting).
    Flexible Use Can be used in WHERE, HAVING, and JOIN conditions.
    Precedence
  • Evaluated after AND but before comparison operators (=, <,>, etc.).
  • OR has lower precedence than AND. Parentheses can be used to explicitly control evaluation order.
    		•
    NULL Handling If one condition evaluates to TRUE, the presence of NULL in another condition does not affect the result.


    Syntax

    SELECT column1 , column2 , . . .
    FROM table_name
      WHERE condition1 OR condition2 ;

    Examples

    Scenario 1:- Retrieve employees who work in department 10 or have a salary greater than 5,000.

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , first_name , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE department_id = 10 OR salary > 5000 ;

    Scenario 2:- Retrieve employees who are either managers, work in department 20, or have a salary greater than 10,000.

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , first_name , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE job_id = 'Manager'
        OR department_id = 20
        OR salary > 10000 ;

    Scenario 3:- Retrieve employees who are either managers (job_id = 'MANAGER') or were hired before 2015.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , job_id , hire_date
    FROM employees
      WHERE job_id = 'Manager'
        OR hire_date < TO_DATE ( ' 2015-01-01', 'YYYY-MM-DD ' ) ;

    Scenario 4:- ( OR with BETWEEN ) Retrieve customers who are located in either 'Hyderabad' or 'Mumbai'.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , hire_date , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE hire_date BETWEEN TO_DATE ( ' 2010-01-01', 'YYYY-MM-DD ' )
        AND TO_DATE ( ' 2015-12-31', 'YYYY-MM-DD ' )
        OR salary BETWEEN 4000 AND 7000 ;

    Scenario 5:- ( OR with NOT ) Find employees who are either not in department 20 or do not have a job ID of 'IT_PROG'.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , department_id , job_id
    FROM employees
      WHERE NOT department_id = 20
      OR NOT job_id = 'IT_PROG' ;

    Scenario 6:- ( OR with Subquery ) Retrieve employees who either work in a department located in New York or have a salary higher than the company average.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , department_id , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE NOT department_id IN (
        SELECT department_id
        FROM departments
          WHERE location_id = (
            SELECT location_id
            FROM locations
              WHERE city = 'New York' )
            )
            OR salary > ( SELECT AVG (salary) FROM employees ) ;

    Scenario 7:- ( OR with CASE ) Classify employees based on whether they meet any priority condition.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name
      CASE
        WHEN department_id = 10 OR salary > 6000 THEN 'High Priority'
        ELSE 'Standard'
      END AS priority_level
    FROM employees ;

    Scenario 8:- ( OR with LIKE ) Find employees whose last name starts with 'A' or 'B'.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , last_name
    FROM employees
      WHERE last_name LIKE 'A%' OR last_name LIKE 'B%' ;

    Scenario 9:- ( OR with GROUP BY and HAVING ) Find departments where the total salary exceeds 50000 or the average salary exceeds 7000.

    SELECT department_id , SUM (salary) AS total_salary , AVG (salary) AS avg_salary
    FROM employees
      GROUP BY department_id
        HAVING SUM (salary) > 50000 OR AVG (salary) > 7000 ;

    Scenario 10:- ( Handling NULL with OR )Retrieve products with a price greater than 100 or those without a price specified (NULL).

    SELECT product_id , product_name , price
    FROM products
      WHERE price > 100 OR price IS NULL ;

    Scenario 11:- ( Using OR with AND ) Retrieve employees who are either in department 30 or earn more than 70,000 in salary but are not managers.

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary , job_title
    FROM employees
    WHERE ( department_id = 30 OR salary > 70000 ) AND department_name != 'Manager' ;

    Scenario 12:- ( OR in JOIN ) Retrieve orders where the customer is located in 'New York' or the shipping address is in 'California'.

    SELECT o.order_id , o.customer_id , c.city AS customer_city , s.state AS shipping_state
    FROM orders o
    JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
    JOIN shipping s ON o.shipping_id = s.shipping_id
      WHERE c.city = 'New York' OR c.city = 'California' ;

    Scenario 13:- ( OR with EXISTS ) Find employees who work in the 'Sales' department or have placed at least one order.

    SELECT employee_id , department_id
    FROM employees
    WHERE department_id = (
      SELECT department_id FROM departments WHERE department_name = 'Sales'
      )
    OR EXISTS ( SELECT 1 FROM orders WHERE orders.employee_id = employees.employee_id ) ;

    Common Errors with OR Operator

    Error Cause Solution
    ORA-00904: Invalid Identifier Misspelling column names or using non-existent columns in conditions. Verify column names in the schema and query.
    ORA-01722: Invalid Number Comparing incompatible data types (e.g., number with text). Ensure data types of columns and values in conditions match appropriately.
    No Rows Returned Overly broad conditions that exclude all rows when combined with conflicting AND. Test conditions incrementally and structure queries logically.


    Performance Considerations Using OR

    • Index Optimization:- Use indexes on columns referenced in the OR conditions to improve performance.
    • Short-Circuiting:- Oracle SQL stops evaluating conditions in an OR clause once it finds one condition to be TRUE. Structure conditions so the most likely TRUE condition appears first.
    • Avoid Overuse:- Excessive use of OR can lead to suboptimal query plans. Consider using IN or rewriting queries if feasible.


    Do's and Don'ts of OR Operator

    Do's Don'ts
    Use parentheses to group conditions when combining AND and OR for proper logical evaluation. Don’t rely on implicit precedence when combining AND and OR; always use parentheses.
    Combine conditions logically to broaden query results. Don’t use OR unnecessarily, as it can impact query performance if not optimized.
    Test each condition individually before combining them with OR. Don’t forget to analyze how OR impacts query performance, especially with large datasets.
    Use OR only when any of the specified conditions may be true. Don’t combine conditions that are logically conflicting or redundant.
