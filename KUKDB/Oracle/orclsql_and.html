    AND in Oracle SQL

    The AND operator in Oracle SQL is a logical operator used to combine two or more conditions in a WHERE clause, HAVING clause, or JOIN conditions.


    It ensures that all the specified conditions must evaluate to TRUE for a row to be included in the result set.


    All the conditions joined by the AND operator must evaluate to TRUE for the row to be included in the query result.



    Syntax

    SELECT column1 , column2 , . . .
    FROM table_name
    WHERE condition1 AND condition2 ;

    Key Features of AND Operator

    Feature Description
    Combines Conditions Combines two or more conditions, all of which must be TRUE for a row to be selected.
    Precedence Evaluated after comparison operators (=, <,>, etc.) and before the OR operator.
    Flexible Use Can be used in WHERE, HAVING, and JOIN clauses, among others.


    Examples

    Scenario 1:- ( Basic AND Condition ) Find employees with a salary greater than 5000 and working in department 10

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE department_id = 10
      AND salary > 50000 ;

    Scenario 2 - ( AND with Multiple Conditions ) :- Find employees who are in department 20, have a job_id of 'IT_PROG', and a salary above 7000.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , job_id , salary , department_id
    FROM employees
      WHERE department_id = 20
      AND job_id = 'IT_PROG'
      AND salary > 7000 ;

    Scenario 3 - ( AND with Date Range ) :- Find employees hired between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2021.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , hire_date
    FROM employees
      WHERE hire_date >= TO_DATE ( '2020-01-01', 'YYYY-MM-DD' )
        AND hire_date <= TO_DATE ( '2021-12-31', 'YYYY-MM-DD' ) ;

    Scenario 4 - ( AND with NOT ):- Find employees who do not work in department 30 and have a salary above 4000.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , salary , department_id
    FROM Students
      WHERE NOT department_id = 30
        AND salary > 4000 ;

    Scenario 5 - ( Filtering Products by Multiple Numeric Ranges ):- Get all students enrolled in the "Mathematics" course with grades above 80.

    SELECT ProductID , Name , Price , Discount
    FROM Products
    WHERE Price BETWEEN 50 AND 500 AND Discount > 10 ;

    Scenario 6 - ( Combining Date and Textual Filters ):- Retrieve events that are scheduled after today and have the status "Confirmed."

    SELECT EventID , Name , EventDate , Status
    FROM Events
      WHERE EventDate > CURRENT_DATE AND Status = 'Confirmed' ;

    Operator Precedence

    When multiple logical operators are used in a query, they are evaluated in the following order of precedence:NOT , AND , OR


    Use parentheses to override the default precedence and clarify intent.


    Example 1:

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE department_id = ' 10 OR salary > 50000 AND NOT job_title = 'Manager' ;
    - - 1. Evaluate NOT job_title = 'Manager'.
    - - 2. Evaluate salary > 50000 AND result_of_NOT.
    - - 3. Evaluate department_id = 10 OR result_of_AND.

    To change precedence:

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary
    FROM employees
      WHERE ( department_id = ' 10 OR salary > 50000 ) AND NOT job_title = 'Manager' ;
    - - 1. Evaluate NOT job_title = 'Manager'.
    - - 2. Evaluate salary > 50000 AND result_of_NOT.
    - - 3. Evaluate department_id = 10 OR result_of_AND.

    Example 2:

    Retrieve employees from department 20 who are either earning more than 60,000 or have a job title other than 'Salesman'.

    SELECT employee_id , department_id , salary , job_title
    FROM employees
      WHERE department_id = ' 20 AND ( salary > 70000 OR NOT job_title = 'Salesman' ) ;
    - - 1. Evaluate NOT job_title = 'Manager'.
    - - 2. Evaluate salary > 50000 AND result_of_NOT.
    - - 3. Evaluate department_id = 10 OR result_of_AND.

    Scenario 7:- ( Combining AND with OR ) Retrieve customers from "USA" or "Canada" who have spent more than $5,000.

    SELECT CustomerID , Name , Country , TotalSpending
    FROM Customers
      WHERE ( Country = ' USA ' OR Country = 'Canada' ) AND TotalSpending > 5000 ;

    Scenario 8:- Find employees who belong to "Sales" or "Marketing" departments and have completed at least two projects.

    SELECT e.EmployeeID , e.Name , e.department_name , COUNT ( p.ProjectID ) AS ProjectCount
    FROM Employees e
    JOIN Projects p ON e.EmployeeID = p.EmployeeID
      WHERE ( e.DepartmentID = ' Sales 'OR e.department_name = ' Marketing ' )
      AND p.Status = ' Completed '
        GROUP BY e.EmployeeID , e.Name , e.department_name
          HAVING COUNT( p.ProjectID ) >= 2 ;

    Scenario 9:- ( AND with Subquery ) Retrieve orders placed by customers who live in "California" and have placed more than three orders.

    SELECT OrderID , CustomerID , OrderDate , Amount
    FROM Orders
    WHERE CustomerID IN (
        SELECT CustomerID
        FROM Orders
        WHERE State = ' California '
        )
    AND OrderCount > 3 ;

    Scenario 10:- ( AND with Subquery ) Find employees working in departments with more than 5 employees.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name , department_id , Salary
    FROM employees
    WHERE department_id IN (
        SELECT department_id
        FROM employees
        GROUP BY department_id
        HAVING COUNT (*) > 5
        )
    AND salary > 3000 ;

    Scenario 11:- ( AND with CASE Expression ) Classify employees with salaries above 5000 in department 10.

    SELECT employee_id , first_name
      CASE
        WHEN salary > 5000 AND department_id = 10 THEN 'High Salary in Dept 10'
        ELSE 'Other'
      END AS classification
    FROM employees ;

    Common Errors with AND Operator

    1. Misusing AND/OR

    Error Cause Solution
    ORA-00904: Invalid Identifier Misspelling column names or using non-existent columns in conditions. Verify column names in the schema and query.
    ORA-01722: Invalid Number Comparing incompatible data types (e.g., number with text). Ensure data types of columns and values in conditions match appropriately.
    No Rows Returned Overly restrictive conditions that result in no rows meeting all criteria. Relax or test conditions incrementally to ensure results.


    Limitations of AND Operator

    Limitations Description
    NULL Handling If any condition evaluates to NULL, the row is excluded from the results, even if other conditions are TRUE.
    Complex Logic Too many AND conditions can make queries hard to read and maintain. Use subqueries or CTEs if needed.
    Performance Impact Complex AND conditions can slow down performance, especially on large datasets without proper indexing.


    Do's and Don'ts of AND Operator

    Do's Don'ts
    Use parentheses to group conditions and control logical evaluation order. Don’t assume AND has higher precedence than OR; always use parentheses for clarity.
    Combine conditions logically to avoid overly complex queries. Don’t combine too many conditions without structuring or commenting for readability.
    Use AND only when all conditions must be satisfied. Don’t use AND when some conditions may conflict (e.g., mutually exclusive conditions).
    Test each condition individually before combining them with AND. Don’t assume all conditions are independent; ensure no redundant or conflicting logic.
